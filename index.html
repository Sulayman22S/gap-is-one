Spectral graph theory, checked in Lean 4

The best constant in λ2 ≤ μ1 − c is 1

Conjecture 2 of T. Cazenave's Lemmas and Conjectures in Spectral Graph Theory says that every connected graph satisfies λ2 ≤ μ1 − 2. It is false. The known bound, with 1 in place of 2, cannot be improved at all.

Sulaymon Sadullo, November 2026

Theorem The inequality λ2(G) ≤ μ1(G) − c holds for every connected graph G with at least 2 vertices if and only if c ≤ 1. λ2 is the second largest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix and μ1 the largest eigenvalue of the Laplacian matrix. Conjecture 2 already fails on a graph with 11 vertices, and on some graph with n vertices for every n ≥ 11.

What is new and what is not

The bound λ2 ≤ μ1 − 1 was known. It follows from λ1 ≤ Δ and the theorem of Grone and Merris (1994) that μ1 ≥ Δ + 1, where Δ is the largest degree, and also from Theorem 1 of Cazenave's paper. The new part, as far as a search of the literature on 18 November 2026 could tell, is that no constant above 1 works. That settles Conjecture 2 and two weaker conjectures of the same corpus, with √2 and the fourth root of 2 in place of 2.

How it was made

A computer search over graphs found the first counterexample. The proofs, the Lean code, the paper and the web pages were written by an AI system (Claude, by Anthropic), directed by the author. Every formal proof is checked by the Lean kernel, and the paper says exactly which statements are covered by Lean and which are proved only by hand.
